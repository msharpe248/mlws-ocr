# Research provenance

> **Historical note (added 2026-08-31).** Several techniques below turn out to
> have direct precedent in the project author's own 1994 system — IDUR
> (M. Sharpe, N. Ahmed & G. Sutcliffe, "An Intelligent Document Understanding
> & Reproduction System," MVA '94, p. 267,
> [PDF](http://b2.cvl.iis.u-tokyo.ac.jp/mva/proceedings/CommemorativeDVD/1994/papers/1994267.pdf)):
> Hough skew correction via the same Hinds et al. (1990) reference our
> `deskew.hough` cites, RLSA (whose noise sensitivity IDUR already noted),
> text/picture/line-drawing block classification by image features (the
> ancestor of our `imagezones`), and Nagy's X-Y trees. mlws-ocr re-derived
> these independently before the paper resurfaced. The author's directional
> k-NN + SCC segmenter (1995, `layout/knn_scc.py`) grew from the same work.

Every algorithm and approach in mlws-ocr, with its lineage, where it lives
in this codebase, and how our implementation deviates. Rule of the house:
**a new algorithm does not land without an entry here and a docstring
naming its source.**

Status: ✅ implemented · 🔬 implemented, superseded or optional · 🗺 planned

## Image cleanup

| Technique | Lineage | Code | Notes / deviations |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ Projection-profile deskew | H.S. Baird, "The Skew Angle of Printed Documents" (1987); W. Postl (1986) | `cleanup/deskew.py` `ProjectionDeskew` | Row-sum variance as the alignment energy; coarse-to-fine search on a downsampled Otsu ink mask instead of Baird's connected-component sampling. |
| ✅ Hough-transform deskew | S.N. Srihari & V. Govindaraju, "Analysis of textual images using the Hough transform" (1989); Hinds, Fisher & D'Amato (1990) | `cleanup/deskew.py` `HoughDeskew` | One reference point per CC (bottom-center ≈ baseline); accumulator scored by Σ(bin²), equivalent to profile variance on points. ~9× faster than raster rotation. |
| ✅ Sauvola adaptive binarization | J. Sauvola & M. Pietikäinen, "Adaptive document image binarization", Pattern Recognition 33 (2000) | `cleanup/binarize.py` `SauvolaBinarize` | Via scikit-image. Known behavior: cuts thinner strokes on sharp clean text than a fixed 0.5 gray threshold (measured; see RESEARCH-NOTES in memory of 2026-08-30 sev-0 study). |
| ✅ Otsu global threshold | N. Otsu, "A threshold selection method from gray-level histograms" (1979) | `cleanup/binarize.py` `OtsuBinarize` | Baseline comparator in the registry. |
| ✅ Illumination flattening by background division | Flat-fielding; morphological background estimation à la S. Sternberg's rolling ball (1983) | `cleanup/illumination.py` | Background = heavy median filter on a downsampled copy, then divide. Median replaces the rolling ball. |
| ✅ Despeckle by component area | Standard document-image practice (see O'Gorman & Kasturi, *Document Image Analysis*, 1995) | `cleanup/despeckle.py` | Area threshold declared at 300 dpi, scaled by (dpi/300)². |

## Layout analysis

| Technique | Lineage | Code | Notes / deviations |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ Recursive XY-cut | G. Nagy & S. Seth, "Hierarchical representation of optically scanned documents" (ICPR 1984) | `layout/blocks.py` | Widest-gap-first axis choice; depth-first traversal order used directly as reading order. |
| ✅ Ruling detection by run-length morphology | Document morphology practice; cf. Wong, Casey & Wahl, "Document Analysis System" (IBM JRD 1982); D. Bloomberg's leptonica writings | `layout/rulings.py` | Opening with 1×L / L×1 elements; rules removed (dilated ×2 — ×1 left stubs) before line finding. Tolerance pass (default on): closing bridges hairline breaks and dilation PERPENDICULAR to the rule direction covers waviness — a 1–2 px rule wandering ±1 px never yields the straight run a strict opening demands. Found via a 400 dpi outlier page that lost its column rules entirely (−8 char); fix took it from 61.0→69.9% and turned the paired 300-vs-400 study into a consistent 4B win (mean 81.2→83.5). Still skew-sensitive ≳1° — noted gap. |
| ✅ Projection-profile line segmentation + baseline estimate | Classic; see survey: R. Cattoni et al., "Geometric layout analysis techniques" (1998) | `layout/lines.py` | Baseline = last row ≥ 25% of peak ink scanning upward (descender-robust heuristic). |
| ✅ Image/halftone zone removal | Text/image segmentation: Wong, Casey & Wahl (IBM JRD 1982); D. Bloomberg, "Multiresolution morphological analysis of document images" (1991); L. Fletcher & R. Kasturi, "A robust algorithm for text string separation from mixed text/graphics images" (PAMI 1988) for the line-art rule | `layout/imagezones.py` | Giant well-filled components + dense coarse-scale regions, union'd, grown, removed from ink before layout. Found via UNLV magazines: photo silhouettes bridge every whitespace channel, XY-cut collapses to one mega-block, and glyphs get union-grouped into photo components (138 groups on a page with ~500 words; 1116 after the stage). Zones must clear a minimum area (min_zone_frac): bold headline glyphs trip the density detector locally and were getting eaten (measured on newsprint). LINE-ART DETECTORS (2026-09-01, from the broad-30 deletion decomposition): (1) large HOLLOW components — big in BOTH dimensions (≥120px each at 300dpi) but sparsely filled, the Fletcher-Kasturi size rule the well-filled test misses (display glyphs and letter-spaced logos are big in one dimension only, so they stay); (2) ZONE ABSORPTION — a component ≥4× median CC dim within 12px of a detected zone is a remnant of the same graphic. Root case (visual session on UNLV 8509): a mailbag illustration's dense half was zoned while its line-art envelope spill stayed, welded two opening paragraphs into one unsplittable "line", and 507 chars of body text vanished into the garbage gate. After: 8509 deletions 507→33 chars; broad-30 char 77.5→78.4 (word flat 52.4, recall +0.8); news-10 75.2 / mag-6 59.8 — both far above their stale baselines, no absorption collateral. Regression fixture: tests/test_imagezones.py::test_line_art_removed_text_kept. |
| ✅ Tall-line adaptive re-split | Profile line finding with adaptive thresholds (cf. projection-profile line segmentation surveys; smoothing per Wong-Casey-Wahl) | `layout/lines.py` (profile impl) | Second deletion-decomposition fix (2026-09-01): on wide blocks the fixed noise floor (0.002×block width) sits BELOW inter-line valleys — descender/ascender overlap plus photocopy speckle keeps 1–2% of a wide row inked — so multiple text lines fuse into one segment whose vertically-stacked glyph groups recognize as i/l/! garbage and get suppressed (UNLV 8718: a 3-line paragraph, valleys 7–38 ink vs floor 3.4). Fix: any line taller than 1.8× the page median height is re-profiled with a threshold adaptive to its own peak (12%), smoothed over 5 rows (raw ascender rows sputter around any fixed cut and shed veto fragments — first attempt failed exactly there), tiny fragments merged into neighbors; a genuinely tall single line (display type, signature scribble) has no deep interior valley and passes unchanged. Results: 8718 deletions 193→0, 8753 169→0, 8509 residual 33→0; broad-30 77.5→78.4 (line art)→78.7 char over the two segmentation legs. |
| ✅ Per-glyph rejection retired as default | Reject-option classification (Chow's rule, 1970: reject when error probability exceeds the rejection cost) — revisited as the classifier improved | `decode/beam.py` `reject_mads=None` | Third deletion-decomposition fix (2026-09-01): the confusion report showed ~600 '?' outputs standing in for common letters. Re-sweep of the adaptive reject threshold (median+k·MAD), which measured FLAT (k 3–10) when introduced, turned strictly monotonic-DOWN with the harvest-strengthened prototypes: dev-8 85.7→88.2 char / 65.2→68.3 word from k=5 to off; broad-30 confirmed +1.6 char/+2.0 word; synthetic +2.7/+3.9/+3.6 char, word sev1 72.3→85.0. Chow's logic inverted: when the classifier was weak, '?' beat its guesses; now every '?' is a guaranteed error while beam+LM's best guess is usually right. Junk-line defense belongs to the garbage-suppression gate (line-level, lexicon+confidence-based), not per-glyph rejection. Mechanism kept as an opt-in parameter; per-char confidences still reported for downstream consumers. |
| ✅ Robust line x-height (caps-line fix) | Bimodal 2-means clustering (same pattern as the gap_band spacing fix); page-level typographic anchoring | `decode/beam.py` `_line_x_height` | Fourth confusion-cluster fix (2026-09-01): case-flip context analysis showed 225 of 519 upper→lower flips inside ALL-CAPS words and most of the 207 word-start flips on caps-heavy lines (letterheads, org names, address lines). Root: per-line x-height = median glyph height, which IS the cap height on such lines, so every capital paid the -1.2 "too-tall lowercase" prior and whole words flipped. Fix: cluster glyph ASCENTS (baseline to top — descenders don't pollute) 2-means per line; bimodal → low mode is the true x-height; unimodal AND ≥1.25× the page's lowercase anchor (median over lines of median ascent — body lines dominate) → caps line, use the anchor. dev-8 +1.3 char/+2.2 word; broad-30 80.3→81.0 char, 54.6→55.9 word; synthetic word +0.9/+1.4/+2.0. Remaining case tail: i→I/b→B word-start flips (pixel-identical twins, Capitalized pattern legal) and sentence-initial caps — needs a sentence-case prior, not size evidence. |
| ✅ Sentence-case repair pass | Orthographic post-correction (case restoration/truecasing lineage, e.g. Lita et al., "tRuEcasIng", ACL 2003) applied ONLY where pixels are silent | `decode/beam.py` `_sentence_case_pass` | Word-level case repair for first letters whose case twins pixels cannot separate (c,o,s,u,v,w,x,z size twins + i/I,b/B,p/P tall pairs): flip UP at a sentence start, flip DOWN a lone Capitalized corpus-frequent word mid-sentence. Three measured hazards shaped the guards: (1) up-flips are DOCUMENT-CALIBRATED — applied only when the document's own unambiguous sentence starts are ≥3:1 capitalized (all-lowercase documents and fixtures must not have style imposed); (2) sentence state across an unpunctuated line break is UNKNOWN, not mid-sentence — no flips there ("Call me Ishmael" at a verse-style line start was flipping down); (3) neighbor-capitalization veto protects proper-noun runs ("San Antonio"), and down-flips require very-frequent lowercase forms (>-10). Net: broad-30 +0.1 char/+0.4 word (81.1/56.3); synthetic word −0.3 avg residual — the irreducible cost is lone ambiguous-initial proper nouns with frequent lowercase twins ("Ishmael"→"ishmael", "Brown"→"brown" when a neighbor misreads); accepted as the real-page/synthetic trade and recorded. |
| ✅ Dehyphenation pass | Standard OCR post-processing (line-break hyphen removal, cf. any dehyphenation heuristic in OCR literature); lexicon-gated | `decode/beam.py` `_dehyphenate_pass` | From the dash-insertion context study (2026-09-01): ground truth reads "indi-\nvidual" as "individual". Two variants: (a) cross-line join (line-final "xxx-", next line of the SAME block starts lowercase, joined core endorsed; the same-block guard correctly refuses paragraph-final period-misread-as-dash + new sentence); (b) token-level (the wrapped halves often arrive already merged as "indi-vidual": strip the internal hyphen when the joined core is a word and neither the hyphenated form nor both halves are — "self-service"/"Michigan-Dearborn" keep theirs). Aggregate-NEUTRAL on broad-30 (cases too rare: precision +0.1, else flat) — kept as a correctness improvement; the rest of the dash cluster is garbled letterhead-region debris (the open letterhead recognition problem), not a dash-specific mechanism. |
| 🔬 knn_scc edge-distance variant | Author's 2026 refinement of his 1995 algorithm | `layout/knn_scc.py` `distance_mode="edge"` | Link length = minimum distance between the two boxes' edge-center points (sector classification stays centroid-based). Measured: ties centroid mode on the fixture (IoU 1.00) and letters; on photo-heavy news it INVERTS the big-component bias rather than removing it — a large remnant's edges are near everything, so it merges INTO text (12 mixed blocks, 54.5% vs centroid's 47/77.4%). Kept as an option; the two biases trade places by domain. Same session: spread-based prune thresholds (mean+kσ, MAD) measured collapsing newspapers (heavy-tailed lengths from photo fragments) — default reverted to the 1995 ratio rule; paper amended with the cross-domain caveat. |
| 🔬 knn_scc per-axis pruning (granularity study) | Docstrum's separate within-line/between-line spacing estimates (O'Gorman, PAMI 1993) motivated splitting the prune threshold by link axis | `layout/knn_scc.py` `prune_scope="per_axis"` / `"per_axis_nn"` | Question (user-raised, paper Fig. 5): why does a letter's body weld into ONE block instead of per-paragraph blocks? RESOLUTION: it doesn't — the published figure was STALE, rendered during the brief mean+1σ default era whose looser cutoff welds the body. At the reverted 1.5×mean spec default the same page (bus.3B 8760_001) gives 11 blocks at paragraph granularity: date, address block, paragraphs, bullets each their own component (two benign welds: salutation+para 1; final tight-pitched bullets). The per-axis variants built to "fix" the phantom weld were still informative: `per_axis` (factor×mean per axis) barely changes the cut; `per_axis_nn` (factor × median per-node NEAREST link) descends BELOW paragraph level into word confetti on sparse lines (103 blocks; verified visually) and shatters letter-spaced display text (fixture title IoU 1.00→0.13). Conclusion: the threshold is a GRANULARITY DIAL — mean+1σ = region level, 1.5×mean = paragraph level, nearest-link = word level — and the near-identical char accuracy of these thresholds (paper §6) is not because they produce the same blocks but because line re-finding within blocks tolerates the level shifting. Default unchanged; scopes kept as options; Fig. 5 regenerated + caption rewritten. LESSON (process): the figure was generated under since-reverted defaults — regenerate figures after any default change. |
| ✅ Whitespace maximal empty rectangles (experimental) | H.S. Baird, "Background structure in document images" (1994); T.M. Breuel, "Two geometric algorithms for layout analysis" (2002) | `layout/whitespace.py` (`blocks.whitespace`; default remains `xycut`) | Branch-and-bound maximal empty rects over RLSA-smeared line segments (glyph-level obstacles fragment spanning headlines — measured); gutters validated by SIDE SUPPORT (ink hugging both flanks ≥0.30 of gutter height; real gutter measured 0.31/0.44, false margins/word-gaps ≤0.15 — an unvalidated version produced 22 false gutters on one newspaper page); banded column-major reading order (spanning blocks close bands). Status: passes the synthetic multicolumn suite, parity with xycut on newspapers (42.1 vs 41.3 char); the recall its better segmentation gains (+9 measured pre-validation) is not yet retained through validation. Trim-to-supported-extent measured WORSE (recall 20.5). A visual-debugging session (2026-08-31) then mapped the full failure space on one newspaper page and banked four findings for a redesign: (1) RIVERS AT GUTTER SCALE — obstacle smear must exceed word gaps (15–25px body, 50–70px display) yet stay under gutter width (~30px): no single length works, so spans need their own long smear; (2) the long smear welds body lines across narrow gutters, so span candidates must also be TALL — and the height median must be computed over line-sized obstacles only (tiny fragments drove the median to 4px and neutered the gate); (3) gutter validation must be flank-ASYMMETRIC — the next column's left edges align under any alignment mode, but the left column's right edges are ragged unless justified; (4) removing span-band obstacles from column bookkeeping deleted body text wholesale when a false span band overlapped columns (aggregate 42→25 char). All variants reverted to the parity implementation; the redesign should be built against SYNTHETIC NEWSPAPER FIXTURES (known gutters/spans/rivers as unit tests) rather than live-page iteration. |
| ✅ Table cell grid from rule intersections | Ruling-based table recognition; R. Zanibbi, D. Blostein & J. Cordy, "A survey of table recognition" (IJDAR 2004) | `layout/tables.py`, `decode/output.py` | Union-find over touching rules groups a frame; clustered rule coordinates give row/column levels; cells are the inter-level rectangles (≥2×2 required — lone separators are not tables). Output stage places decoded words into cells by box center → meta.tables_text grid. Side discovery: language detection on a near-empty page (9 isolated words) confidently picked Italian — detection now requires ≥60 chars of pseudo-text and otherwise falls back to a default language. |

## Glyph features (`glyph/features.py`)

| Feature | Lineage | Notes / deviations |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ Zoning densities (8×8) | Classic hand-print OCR feature; surveyed in Ø.D. Trier, A.K. Jain & T. Taxt, "Feature extraction methods for character recognition — a survey", Pattern Recognition 29 (1996) | Our single strongest feature family (measured +4–5 pts top-1 over 4×4). |
| ✅ Hu invariant moments | M.-K. Hu, "Visual pattern recognition by moment invariants" (1962) | Log-compressed to tame dynamic range. |
| ✅ Crossing counts | Classic; Trier et al. (1996) | 4 rows + 4 columns of ink-run counts. |
| ✅ Skeleton endpoints/junctions | Thinning: T.Y. Zhang & C.Y. Suen (1984) via scikit-image `skeletonize` | Counts only in the feature vector; the full structural representation lives in `glyph/skeleton.py` (attributed graphs: endpoint/junction nodes with positions and degrees, stroke edges with length/angle/straightness, explicit loop count; rings like 'o' become a self-loop node). |
| ✅ Gated GED reranking | K. Riesen & H. Bunke, "Approximate graph edit distance computation by means of bipartite graph matching" (IVC 2009); structural OCR lineage per Kahan-Pavlidis-Baird | `recognize/ged.py`, `recognize/stage.py`, `scripts/build_skeletons.py` | Bipartite-assignment GED over skeleton graphs as a SECOND OPINION on the feature matcher's top-6, active only behind two gates, both measured necessary: (1) roughness < 0.72 — flip-noise corrupts skeletons (ungated: −5 on heavy isolated glyphs; fixes 7, breaks 12); (2) feature top-1/2 margin < 25% — blur corrupts skeletons while SMOOTHING roughness, and ungated GED overrode confident correct calls (synthetic sev2 −13 word). Three further lessons completed it: (3) bank-uncovered candidates need the NEUTRAL mean GED — rescoring only covered classes penalized them relatively, and accent classes with no skeletons flooded top-1 ("the"→"tnü"); (4) the bank must be OPERATOR-CONSISTENT — clean 48px prototypes mismatch Sauvola-thinned 32px pipeline crops (an 'e' whose bar vanished needs a matching degraded prototype), so the bank carries 48px/32px/blurred-32px variants; (5) scale swept to 10 (25 taxed synthetic). FINAL: broad 30-page letters 75.7 char/44.2 word (+1.1/+2.1), dev-8 84.2/55.5, synthetic at parity — a clean net win, largest in the clean-to-light regime where customer grayscale PDFs live. Language detection gained a challenger margin (default holds unless beaten by 0.08) after noisy top-1 text flipped borderline en/de. |
| ✅ Persistence-graded hole counts | Simplified from persistent homology (H. Edelsbrunner, J. Harer, *Computational Topology*, 2010) | Holes counted after closing at radii {0,1,2}: a hairline-broken 'o' keeps its hole at r≥1. Poor man's persistence — no filtration diagram. |
| ✅ Moment-based deslanting | Slant normalization from handwriting recognition; cf. Bozinovic & Srihari (1989) | Shear by μ11/μ02, clipped to ±0.6. Lets one prototype cover roman + italic (+3 pts shape-class top-1). |
| ✅ Stroke-width normalization | Cf. Tesseract's character normalization (R. Smith, "An overview of the Tesseract OCR engine", ICDAR 2007) | Erode/dilate toward width = 0.16·max(h,w), estimated from the distance transform median. |
| ✅ Side profiles (left/right first-ink distance) | Profile features; Trier et al. (1996) | Separates b/d, p/q. |

## Recognition, decoding, language

| Technique | Lineage | Code | Notes / deviations |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ Nearest-prototype (1-NN, z-scored) | T. Cover & P. Hart, "Nearest neighbor pattern classification" (1967) | `recognize/nearest.py` | Std floored at 5% of mean std — a raw ε amplified near-constant features into the dominant distance term (measured catastrophic). Candidate depth matters far more than expected: top-k 5→8→10 gained +2 then +4 char on real scans (the truth for unseen fonts often sits at rank 6–10, where the beam+LM+adaptation stack can still find it); 12 plateaus. Beam width does NOT follow: 16/24 measured slightly worse than 8 even with 10 candidates — wider beams surface LM-favored wrong paths. |
| ✅ Synthetic training data via font rendering + degradation | H.S. Baird, "Document image defect models" (1992); T. Kanungo et al., "A statistical, nonparametric methodology for document degradation model validation" (PAMI 2000) | `factory/synth.py` | Kanungo edge-distance-decaying flips + blur + skew + illumination field + mirrored-page bleed-through. Every sample keeps its θ. Bitonal variant added 2026-08-31 (blur → hard threshold, the fax/bitonal-scanner pipeline per Baird's defect model): prototype variants at threshold 0.4/0.55 improved BOTH real scans (+2 char) and synthetic (+2–6 word) — a general augmentation, not just domain matching. |
| ✅ Over-segmentation + LM-scored variant choice | Segmentation survey: R.G. Casey & E. Lecolinet, "A survey of methods and strategies in character segmentation" (PAMI 1996); document decoding: G. Full DID-style lattice not implemented. |
| ✅ Character n-gram beam decoding | C.E. Shannon (1948) n-grams; beam search standard practice | `decode/beam.py` | Trigram over prefix context; add-one smoothing. |
| ✅ Corpus-frequency lexicon + correction | OCR post-correction practice; cf. K. Kukich, "Techniques for automatically correcting words in text" (ACM Surveys 1992) | `lang/model.py`, `decode/beam.py` | Tiered: corpus top-30k weighted + dict-words (≥3 chars) at a floor. Flat word lists measurably hurt (junk 2-letter entries). |
| ✅ Dictionary-endorsed word segmentation | Word-boundary DP practice (cf. Kukich 1992 §segmentation) | `decode/beam.py` `_best_gap_variant` | Key invariant found the hard way: beam score can never be the primary key for split choice — every extra word resets the n-gram context, so shredding always wins locally. Lexicon endorsement is primary; scores break ties. |
| ✅ Bimodal gap clustering for spaces | Standard bimodality assumption on inter-word vs inter-letter gaps (layout surveys, op. cit.) | `decode/beam.py` `gap_band` | Per-line 1-D 2-means; x-height ratio only as fallback (median-of-heights x-height is ascender-inflated — measured twice). |
| ✅ Case disambiguation from line geometry | Typographic-context practice in classic OCR | `decode/beam.py` `_height_prior` | Plus orthographic prior: capital-after-lowercase penalty. Dotted i/j are ascender-tall — missing this cost ~10 char pts (bug of 2026-08-30). |
| ✅ Shape-confusion twin injection | Classic OCR confusion-pair handling (n/h, c/e); same mechanism as case-twin injection | `decode/beam.py` CONFUSION_PAIRS | When one of a confusion pair is a candidate, the other joins at penalty 1.6 so language context arbitrates. Motivating diagnosis: a typewriter letter where 'h' ranked below top-14 while 'n' led — "the" (19 page occurrences) decoded as "LEe"/"CLe", unreachable by any LM because 'h' was never offered. Worst page 75.3→79.5 char; letters set +1.5 char/+4.0 word. Penalty calibrated two-objective (1.0 cost degraded synthetic −3.9 word; 1.6 keeps both). Digit-mode fix in same leg: a letter pin must not override digits ("1993"→"l993" via an 'l' pin). e↔o added next leg after a sans-font page decoded "president"→"prosidonl" throughout ('e' bar fills/vanishes): letters +1.6 char/+5.5 word AND synthetic up — the strongest single pair so far. e↔s and 9↔s followed ("Drivs", "5ss5"=5995): letters +0.3 char/+1.3 word, word acc crossing 50%. Same leg fixed a plain coverage hole: '&', '$', '%', '/', '#' were absent from the class set entirely — "&" decoded as 'a' forever in a corpus full of business text. |
| ✅ Digit-mode decoding (rank-graded, categorical twins) | Numeric-field handling practice in forms OCR | `decode/beam.py` `_beam_word` | Digit evidence graded over candidate ranks (rank-1 = 1.0, ranks 2–3 = 0.6); in digit mode the letter LM is muted, lexicon substitution off, digit candidates boosted, letter pins do not apply, and DIGIT_TWINS (l/I→1, o→0, s→5, z→2, B→8, g→9…) are injected. Boost arithmetic alone lost when adaptation left short sharp candidate lists ('l' beat '1' by 1.5 nats on 5 entries — unit-reproduced), so the rule is categorical: a top-scoring letter with a digit twin in contention yields to the twin. "l993"→"1993", "357l"→"3571", "92l23"→"92123". Letters +1.5 word; ~0.4 synthetic char tax accepted. |
| ✅ doc_type layout priors | Original plan scope (optional hint, never required) | `cli.py --doc-type`, `layout/blocks.py` | Hint flows via page.meta. Single-column prior (letter/book/legal): column-split gap requirement rises 2.5×. Measured +0.4 char on letters — and +6.0 char / +9.6 word on legal pleadings, where double-spaced monospace text is full of vertical whitespace RIVERS (aligned character cells stack spaces into full-height channels) that shredded pages into ~187 single-line blocks. Auto river-defense added: a page shredding past 80 blocks retries the cut at the wide gap — legal unhinted recovers 67.2→70.5 char (hint still best at 73.2). |
| ✅ Corpus confusion methodology | ISRI-style error analysis at character-alignment level | `scripts/confusion_report.py` | Edit-distance backtrace over the full 30-page sample aggregates substitutions/insertions/deletions — the scaled version of the worst-page play; statistics nominate fixes. First run surfaced: ~550 case flips, p→D ×144, punctuation→letters ×280, and net ~700 spacing errors as the largest op category. |
| ✅ Baseline-descender prior | Baseline-relative typographic context (classic zone analysis: ascender/x-height/descender bands) | `decode/beam.py` | A glyph whose box crosses the line baseline is a descender letter (p/y/g/q, not P/Y/D/9), and vice versa — geometry always computed (lines stage baselines) but never consulted at decode. With the tiny-glyph punctuation prior (a mark under 40% of x-height prefers .,'- over letters), broad 30-page letters +1.4 char / +2.7 word and synthetic sev-1 +3.4 word. |
| ✅ Case-pattern grammar | Orthographic convention (words are all-lower, Capitalized, or ALL-CAPS); cf. truecasing literature (Lita et al. 2003) | `decode/beam.py` | Any case transition inside a word pays a penalty except upper→lower right after the first letter. Found via error decomposition on UNLV: case-folding alone moved word acc 16.6→28.6% on the worst page — random per-glyph flips inside caps words were the top word killer. |
| ✅ Garbage-line suppression (shape-gated) | Reject-class practice; cf. handwriting/signature discrimination in letter processing | `decode/output.py` | A line is suppressed only when THREE tests agree: zero lexicon words, mean confidence <0.15, AND degenerate shape (one character ≥40% of the line — "IIIIIIxIIIII"). The original two-test version deleted misread-but-real lines on hard fonts ("ADril 5, 19s31" = April 5, 1991; addresses; "Sincerely"), silently costing whole lines of recall; the shape gate returned them (+1.7 char on letters). |
| 🔬 Graphic-line suppression (distance-based, partial) | Non-text discrimination via recognition confidence | `decode/beam.py`, `decode/output.py`, `adapt/cluster_refit.py` | Attempted: flag lines whose pass-1 median top-1 distance ≫ page median (logo shapes match no prototype), suppress unless a ≥4-char endorsed word survives; graphic-suspect lines excluded from adaptation. KEY DISCOVERY en route: adaptation PINS logos — repeated identical strokes are the most self-consistent shapes on a page and rescored to distance ~0.0 (measured), the purest error-amplification case; the pass-1-only rule and adaptation exclusion are kept as guards. But the relative test fails exactly where needed: stylized-font pages inflate the page median so logo lines (1.2–1.5×) hide below real-line ratios elsewhere (1.3–1.4×). Open: needs absolute distance calibration or shape-level non-text features (density/periodicity), not a page-relative ratio. |

## External benchmark (2026-08-31)

Same 30-page UNLV bus.3B sample, same harness and metrics
(`scripts/eval_tesseract.py`):

| system | char acc | word acc |
|---|---|---|
| Tesseract 5.5 LSTM (`--oem 3`) | 95.9% | 92.2% |
| Tesseract legacy pre-neural (`--oem 0`) | 95.3% | 90.5% |
| **mlws-ocr** (feature+GRU stack, day 2) | **77.1%** | **47.3%** |

Newspapers (10pg): Tesseract LSTM 85.0/81.6; magazines (6pg): 73.3/63.6.
Reading: the legacy engine proves the pre-neural architecture reaches 95%+
on this corpus — the project's founding premise is validated by its own
benchmark rival. The 18-point gap is implementation maturity, chiefly
(a) classifier training data: legacy Tesseract's shape classifier was
trained on enormous real-glyph corpora versus our 26 synthetic fonts;
(b) its character chopper/associator (a full segmentation search we
approximate with single-cut variants); (c) two decades of accumulated
edge-case handling. Historical context: ISRI's 1996 annual test reported
top commercial engines around 97–99% char on these very sets.

## Design lineage (whole-system)

- The overall architecture — explicit features, prototype matching, adaptive
  per-document classification, LM-scored decoding — is the classic pre-neural
  OCR stack, best publicly documented in R. Smith's Tesseract papers (2007,
  2009 "Adapting the Tesseract open source OCR engine for multilingual OCR").
  This project is a from-scratch, legibility-first reimplementation of those
  ideas, not a port.
- Stage/registry/DebugBundle pipeline design and the "manufacture the labels"
  program (synthetic θ-degradation + printed calibration sheet) are original
  to this project's plan; the θ-degradation half follows Baird/Kanungo.
